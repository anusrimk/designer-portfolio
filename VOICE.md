# Anusri Karmokar — Voice Guide

Use this when writing anything in Anusri's name: portfolio case studies, bios, LinkedIn posts, cover notes, project descriptions. Paste the whole file into Claude or ChatGPT before asking for copy (a ready-made prompt is at the bottom).

---

## 1. Who's talking

**Anusri Karmokar — product designer and UX strategist, based in Mumbai.**

- She is a **designer**. Full stop. Not a "designer who codes", not a "designer and developer".
- She *used to* be a developer (she's studying for a B.Tech) and has **already made the switch** to design. If her technical background comes up at all, it's past tense and in service of the design work: *"a developer turned product designer"*, *"my engineering background means I design things that can actually be built"*.
- Never position her as someone who does both jobs. Readers should think "great designer", not "why would I hire one person for two roles?"

**Where she's worked:** JavaScript Mumbai (UI/UX Designer, 2026–present), Momentum Health Club (Product Designer, ongoing), Winvesta (UI/UX Lead, 2025), ITM Business School (UX & Technology Trainer, 2025), LetsUpgrade / 12thClass.com (UX & Product Intern, 2024).

**What she's best at:** design where the stakes are real — money moving across borders, people deciding whether to show up — and making complicated things feel simple without hiding what matters.

## 2. Who's listening

| Reader | What they want in ~30 seconds |
|---|---|
| Hiring managers and design leads | How she thinks, what she decided, what changed because of it |
| Founders and small teams | Can she take something messy and ship something clear? |
| Recruiters | Role, scope, results, and that she's a designer |
| Community and peers (LinkedIn, events) | A real person with a point of view |

Write for someone skimming. The first sentence of anything should carry the most important thing.

## 3. Voice attributes

### Specific
- **Is:** concrete nouns, real numbers, named things. "700+ registrations in a single week."
- **Isn't:** vague process words. "Drove engagement across touchpoints."
- **Sounds like:** "Users were dropping off at KYC because nobody knew what the term meant."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "I conducted research to identify pain points in the user journey."

### Warm, a little self-aware
- **Is:** first person, conversational, the occasional wink at herself.
- **Isn't:** jokey in every line, or using humour to dodge substance. One wink per piece is plenty.
- **Sounds like:** "And yes, I probably still have 47 untitled Figma drafts open."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "lol designing is sooo hard 😅"

### Confident, not inflated
- **Is:** says what she did plainly, and credits the team where it was a team.
- **Isn't:** superlatives, "passionate", "rockstar", or claiming results she can't back up.
- **Sounds like:** "I redesigned onboarding, which contributed to a 15%+ rise in onboardings."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "I single-handedly revolutionised the onboarding experience."

### Clear over clever
- **Is:** short sentences, one idea each. Plain words.
- **Isn't:** corporate filler or design-theory jargon used to sound smart.
- **Sounds like:** "Financial products are complex by nature."
- **Doesn't sound like:** "Financial ecosystems inherently present multifaceted complexity paradigms."

## 4. How to write a case study

Every case study follows this order. Headings are title case.

1. **Intro (1–2 short paragraphs).** What the product is, her role, and **the headline result in the first paragraph** if there is one.
2. **The Problem.** What was actually wrong, from the user's side. One sharp question is a nice touch: *"How do we make a complicated financial process feel simple without hiding important information?"*
3. **My Approach.** Not a list of methods. Use the shape **finding → decision**: *"Mapping the journey showed X, so I did Y."* Never list "personas, goals, pain points, journeys…" — every designer does those.
4. **Visual sections** (User Journey, Low-Fidelity, From Flow to Final UI…). One or two sentences each; let the images talk. Don't publish "coming soon" placeholders — leave the section out until the artwork exists.
5. **Outcome.** Always called "Outcome". What changed: a number, a shipped thing, a behaviour. If there's no metric, name the concrete change that shipped.

**Label honestly.** Concepts, hackathon projects and self-initiated work say so in the meta line ("Concept · Web Experience · …"). Reviewers trust a portfolio more when it's clear what was real.

**Metrics rules.**
- Say what she designed that produced the number: *"Designed the funding flows that processed $1.2M+"*, not *"Worked on $1.2M+ volume"*.
- Give a time frame when there is one.
- Only use a company's internal figures if they're public or she has permission.

## 5. Style rules

| Rule | Use |
|---|---|
| Spelling | **British**: colour, personalised, organised, realise, behaviour, analysing |
| Person | First person ("I"). "We" only when it was genuinely a team decision |
| Oxford comma | Yes: "attendees, developers, speakers, and the wider community" |
| Em dash | Spaced: "Anusri — a developer turned product designer". Use sparingly; a colon or full stop is often better |
| Headings | Title Case ("My Approach", "From Flow to Final UI") |
| Numbers | Numerals with "+" for minimums: 700+, 15%+, $1.2M+, 70+ |
| Dates | "May 2025 — December 2025", "March 2026 — Present" |
| Lists | Only for things that are genuinely a list (deliverables, features). No full stops on fragments |
| Contractions | Yes (I'm, didn't, it's) — it keeps the voice human |
| Exclamation marks | Almost never. Emoji: none in portfolio copy |

## 6. Words

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| designed, built, shipped, mapped, sketched, tested | leveraged, spearheaded, orchestrated, drove |
| simple, clear, straightforward | easy (it's only easy for some people) |
| users, members, students, salespeople (be specific) | end-users, stakeholders (unless literally stakeholders) |
| product designer, UX strategist | designer-developer, full-stack designer, "I also code" |
| what changed | "ensuring", "seamless", "robust", "cutting-edge", "journey" as a buzzword |
| "Contributed to" when it was a team result | claiming team results as solo wins |

**Cut on sight:** passionate, innovative, synergy, holistic, best-in-class, world-class, rockstar, ninja, "in today's fast-paced world", "transformed into a modern digital journey", "overall", "various", "in order to".

## 7. Examples from her own site

**Leading with the result**
> ✗ I work across the digital experience of JavaScript Mumbai, leading website design, visual systems, and digital campaigns. My role sits between UX, visual design, and development, ensuring that the experience feels consistent from the first interaction to registration.
>
> ✓ I design everything a JavaScript Mumbai member touches online: the website, the visual system, and the campaigns that get people through the door. One of those campaigns brought in 700+ registrations in a single week.

**Plain over corporate**
> ✗ The project explores how a traditional banking experience can be transformed into a more modern, approachable digital journey.
>
> ✓ It explores how a traditional bank's website could feel more modern and approachable.

**Designer, not designer-and-developer**
> ✗ I'm Anusri — a designer who codes, a developer who cares way too much about spacing.
>
> ✓ I'm Anusri — a developer turned product designer. I made the switch once I realised I cared *way too much about spacing*.

## 8. Before you hand copy back, check

- [ ] Does the first sentence carry the most important thing?
- [ ] Is every claim specific, and is every number attributed to something she designed?
- [ ] Does anything suggest she's a developer *now*? Remove it or make it past tense.
- [ ] Any word from the "avoid" list?
- [ ] British spelling throughout?
- [ ] Would she say this out loud to a design lead over coffee?
